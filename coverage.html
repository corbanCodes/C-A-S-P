Where we work

All 58 California counties. One inspector, one report format, one calendar — whether your buildings are in one city or nine.

By region

Grouped the way scheduling actually works. Travel outside the immediate region is quoted up front, never added later.

San Francisco Bay Area

Dense commercial frontage, a long tail of pre-1990 storefronts on tight lots, and one of the highest concentrations of construction-related accessibility filings in the state.

Coverage detail

Sacramento & the Capital Region

State offices, a wide band of garden-style apartment stock from the 1970s and 80s, and the wood-framed walkways that come with it.

Coverage detail

Central Valley

Fast-growing multifamily construction alongside older main-street retail that has never been formally assessed.

Coverage detail

Los Angeles County

The largest concentration of apartment buildings in California and the deepest pool of accessibility litigation. SB 721 exposure here is measured in tens of thousands of buildings.

Coverage detail

Orange County

Heavy condominium and common interest development stock, which puts SB 326 board duties near the top of the agenda.

Coverage detail

Inland Empire

Rapid multifamily growth, large HOA communities and long distances between inspectors — scheduling matters more here than almost anywhere.

San Diego County

Central Coast

Multi-property portfolios

If you hold buildings across several regions, the usual problem is not the inspection — it is ending up with six different vendors, six report formats and six renewal dates that nobody is tracking.

We inspect the whole portfolio on one schedule, deliver one format, and hand back a single register of what was found and when each property comes round again. For management companies that is the difference between a compliance programme and a recurring fire drill.
