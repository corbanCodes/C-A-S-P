How an inspection works

From the first call to the report on your desk, and the calendar entry that stops the next cycle sneaking up on you.

Call or send the form

Tell us the property type, the address and which mandate you are dealing with. If you are not sure which one applies to you, that is a normal question and the call is free.

Scope and quote

We confirm square footage, unit counts, how many exterior elevated elements exist and what standards apply based on the building's age and permit history. You get a flat price before anyone schedules anything.

The inspection

We walk the property with instruments, not impressions. Most single-tenant commercial sites take a few hours; multifamily EEE inspections scale with unit count.

The report

A written report you can hand to a lawyer, a board, a lender or a code official. Itemised findings, photographs, the applicable standard for each item and a schedule of completion.

Corrections

You fix what the report lists, using your own contractors. We do not sell the repairs we recommend, which is the entire reason our findings are worth reading.

Re-inspection and the calendar

We verify the corrections, document the corrected condition, and put your next cycle date on a calendar so the six or nine year clock does not run out unnoticed.

We do not sell the repairs we recommend. An inspector with a repair crew has a reason to lengthen the list. We inspect and we report; the corrections are yours to bid competitively. It costs us upsell revenue and it is why a lender, a board or a court can take the report at face value.

What lands on your desk

A report written to be used by somebody else — your attorney, your board, your lender, your contractor or a code official.

Sample CASp report Sample SB 721 report

Itemised findings

Every item listed separately with the applicable standard or code section beside it, not a narrative you have to interpret.

Photographs

Located and captioned, so a contractor bidding the work knows exactly what and where without a second site visit.

Schedule of completion

A date against each correction. Required by Civil Code §55.53 on the CASp side, and simply good practice on the structural side.

Measurements

Actual dimensions recorded against the required ones, so a disputed item can be checked rather than argued.

Certification

Signed by the certified or licensed individual the relevant statute requires — which is the part that gives the document its legal weight.

Your next date

Six years for SB 721, nine for SB 326, and a sensible re-look interval on the accessibility side. Diarised before we leave.

What we need from you

  • The property address and, for multifamily, the unit count
  • Roughly when it was built, and whether it has been remodelled
  • Any permit history or prior reports you already hold
  • Access — keys, codes, and a contact who can open locked areas
  • For HOAs, whoever on the board is authorised to receive the report
  • If you have been served, the date of service. It starts a clock.

Request a quote

Tell us the property and we’ll come back with a flat price. No hourly surprises.

We reply the same business day. We do not sell or share your details, and we do not sell the repairs we recommend.
